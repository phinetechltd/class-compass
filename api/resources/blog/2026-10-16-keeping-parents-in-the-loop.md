Parents want to know three things: is my child in school, how are they doing, and what do I owe? In many schools the answers arrive through a mix of class WhatsApp groups, phone calls to the office, printed circulars that never leave the school bag, and a queue at the bursar's window.

A parent portal does not replace a good relationship between teacher and parent. It removes the routine questions so that relationship has room to grow.

![The Class Compass parent portal: dashboard and fee statement](/assets/classcompass/blog/parent-flow.png)

## What parents should see in one place

- **Attendance.** Today's register and the term's attendance percentage. If a learner is marked absent, the parent should know the same morning, not at the end of the month.
- **Results and report cards.** CBC assessments as they're entered, and the report card at the end of term.
- **Homework and timetable.** What's due, and when lessons and exams are.
- **Fees.** The balance per term, every receipt, and a button to pay by M-Pesa.
- **Notices and events.** Sports days, parent meetings and half-term dates, in one list rather than scattered across chats.

## Why groups don't scale

WhatsApp groups work for a quick reminder, but they cause real problems:

- Important notices get buried under replies.
- Parents in several groups (one per child) miss things.
- Personal matters, such as a fee balance or a discipline case, can't go in a group.
- When a teacher leaves, the group's history often goes with them.

A portal sends each family only what concerns their children, and it stays with the school.

## Getting parents to actually use it

Most portals fail at adoption, not technology. What works:

1. **Hand out logins at an event.** Set up accounts during admission or at a parents' meeting, and help parents sign in on the spot.
2. **Use it for something parents want.** Fee balances and report cards bring parents in. Publish them in the portal first.
3. **Keep it fast on cheap phones.** If it takes a minute to load over mobile data, parents give up.
4. **Keep sending SMS for urgent things.** An SMS when a learner is absent, or when a payment is received, reaches every parent, smartphone or not.

## One login for the whole family

A parent with three children at the school should sign in once and switch between them. Learners get their own login too, for homework, timetables and results.
