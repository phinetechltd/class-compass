Every school in Kenya holds personal data: learners' names and dates of birth, parents' phone numbers and ID numbers, medical notes, discipline records, photos and fee balances. Under the **Data Protection Act, 2019**, the school is responsible for how that data is collected, used, stored and shared, even when it lives in a system run by someone else.

This isn't legal advice, but it covers the principles every head teacher and board should know.

> For your school's specific obligations, including whether you must register with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC), check the ODPC's guidance or ask a legal adviser.

## The principles, in school terms

- **Collect only what you need.** An admission form needs a parent's phone number. It probably doesn't need their employer's address.
- **Use data only for the reason you collected it.** Phone numbers collected for school communication shouldn't be handed to a uniform supplier for marketing.
- **Keep it accurate.** Update contacts every year. Wrong numbers mean absence alerts go to the wrong person.
- **Keep it secure.** Only people who need data should be able to see it.
- **Don't keep it forever.** Agree how long you keep records of learners who have left, and what happens to them after that.
- **Children's data needs extra care.** Get parents' consent before publishing photos of learners, on the website, in ads or on social media.

## Who should see what

Most data leaks in schools are not hacks. They are spreadsheets emailed to the wrong person, or a shared login everyone knows. Good access control means:

- **Every staff member has their own login.** No shared "office" account.
- **Each role sees only what it needs.** The bursar sees fees, not discipline notes. A teacher sees their own classes, not the school's bank settings. A librarian sees the library.
- **Parents see only their own children.** Learners see only themselves.
- **Accounts are closed when people leave.**

In Class Compass, permissions are set per role and checked by the server on every request, not just hidden in the menu. A teacher's login can't open payroll even by typing the address directly.

## Simple habits that protect data

1. **Stop emailing spreadsheets of learner data.** Share access in the system instead.
2. **Use strong, separate passwords** and never write them on the staffroom notice board.
3. **Lock screens** on office computers when you step away.
4. **Check consent before posting photos** of learners, especially in marketing.
5. **Ask your system provider** where data is stored, how it is backed up, and how you can export it if you ever leave.

## Make it part of the culture

Data protection works best as a habit, not a policy document in a drawer. Mention it at the start-of-year staff meeting, include it in new-staff induction, and tell parents how their data is used and protected. They'll trust the school more for it.
